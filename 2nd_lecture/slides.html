Model routing — the “Auto” model

One prompt in — and something outside the model decides which model actually answers it.

  • Copilot, Cursor and others offer Auto: you do not choose the model, a router does — per request. A cheap classifier outside the model, reading the surface of your prompt; it has not thought about your problem.
  • Not the same router as in MoE (lecture 1): that one picks experts inside one model, per token, and was trained. This one picks a whole model, per request — a pricing decision.
  • Plan first — you will not one-shot a real problem. Ask the strongest model for a numbered plan and nothing else (“do not change any files yet”), read it like a referee, keep it in plan.md, then spend one cheap prompt per step. That planning turn is where the 10× multiplier pays for itself.

Prompting tips

The model cannot see your screen, your data, or what you meant. Everything it knows about your problem, you put there.

  • Write in English — fewer tokens per word, better-trained behaviour — but keep local things local: Czech comments, labels and column names where they belong.
  • Drop the politeness. “Could you please…”, “I was wondering if…” are tokens, not manners. Imperative, then the facts.
  • A question can be read as an order. “Why is this loop so slow?” will get you a rewritten loop. If you only want an answer, say “do not change any files, just explain”.
  • Paste the real thing — the command and the whole traceback, not your summary. Say what you already tried, or it will try it again.
  • Say what must not change — “keep the query”, “do not touch the tests”. Everything you do not fence off is fair game.
  • One task per conversation. Stale context from the previous problem is the most common cause of a stupid answer — start a new chat when you switch tasks.
